ARTICLES & QUANTIFIERS
"Từ Nhỏ, Sức Mạnh Lớn"

Bạn có biết rằng việc dùng sai "a" hay "the" có thể khiến câu nói từ "chuyên nghiệp" thành "nghiệp dư"? Đây chính là những "chi tiết nhỏ" tạo nên sự khác biệt lớn!

4.1

Indefinite Article — Mạo Từ Không Xác Định A / AN

🤔 Mở đầu: "A book" hay "An book"?
Bản chất: A/An như "ngón tay chỉ" — chỉ vào một cái gì đó không cụ thể, lần đầu nhắc đến.

📝 Quy tắc cơ bản:

Dùng A
A
Trước âm phụ âm
a university — /ju:/ là phụ âm!
a one-way street — /wʌn/ là phụ âm!
Dùng AN
AN
Trước âm nguyên âm
an hour — /aʊə/ là nguyên âm!
an honest person — /ɒn/ là nguyên âm!

🎯 Cách dùng:

  • Với danh từ đếm được số ít (không cụ thể hoặc lần đầu nhắc đến): There's a good film on TV tonight. Phân tích: Lần đầu nhắc đến bộ phim này!
⚠️ LỖI SAI THƯỜNG GẶP
Lỗi 1 — Nhầm lẫn âm và chữ cái:
❌an unique experience
(âm /ju:/ là phụ âm — dùng A)
✅a unique experience
✅an hour
(âm /aʊə/ là nguyên âm — dùng AN)
Lỗi 2 — Dùng với danh từ không đếm được:
❌I need a water.
✅I need some water.
💡 Mẹo Ghi Nhớ — Nghe Âm, Không Nhìn Chữ!
  • Đọc to từ đó ra → Âm đầu phụ âm = A, âm đầu nguyên âm = AN
  • Không phụ thuộc vào chữ cái — phụ thuộc vào âm phát ra!
4.2

Definite Article — Mạo Từ Xác Định THE

🤔 Mở đầu: Khi nào "the" trở thành "thần kỳ"?
Bản chất: "The" như "ngón tay chỉ chính xác" — chỉ vào thứ cụ thể, cả người nói và nghe đều biết.

🎯 Các cách dùng:

  • 1. Với danh từ đếm được số ít (cụ thể): Where's the DVD you were talking about? Phân tích: DVD cụ thể mà chúng ta đã nói đến!
  • 2. Nói chung về một loại (số ít): The radio seemed amazing to people at first. Ý nghĩa: Radio nói chung, như một phát minh
  • 3. Với danh từ đếm được số nhiều (cụ thể): I didn't believe the rumours about the prime minister.
  • 4. Với danh từ không đếm được (cụ thể): I followed the advice my lawyer gave me.

🔊 Cách phát âm:

Trước phụ âm
/ðə/
the media, the programme
Trước nguyên âm
/ði:/
the announcer, the editor
🌟 Những Thứ Duy Nhất Trên Thế Giới — Luôn Dùng "THE"
  • the sky, the sun, the moon
  • the Prince of Wales, the World Cup
4.3

Zero Article — "Không Mạo Từ"

🤔 Mở đầu: Khi nào "không có gì" lại là "tất cả"?
Bản chất: Đôi khi không dùng mạo từ nào cả — điều này cũng có ý nghĩa!

🎯 Các cách dùng:

  • 1. Danh từ đếm được số nhiều (nói chung): Journalists often face dangerous situations. Ý nghĩa: Các nhà báo nói chung — không cụ thể
  • 2. Danh từ không đếm được (nói chung): News travels fast these days. Ý nghĩa: Tin tức nói chung
4.4

Articles Trong Các Cụm Từ Cố Định — Fixed Expressions

🕐 Thời gian:

Có "the" Không có mạo từ
in the 1990s in 2008
in the summer in June
in the morning on Friday
at night

👥 Người và công việc:

Có "a/an" Có "the" Không có mạo từ
have a job the queen become president
work as a teacher the French (người Pháp) go to work
he's French

🏔️ Địa danh:

Có "the" Không có mạo từ
the Alps (dãy núi) Mount Everest (đỉnh núi)
the River Thames Paris (thành phố)
the USA, the UK America
the Cyclades (quần đảo) Crete (đảo đơn lẻ)

🎵 Giải trí và thể thao:

Có "the" Không có mạo từ
the media listen to music
on the radio on television
play the guitar play tennis
go to the cinema
watch (the) TV

🇺🇸🇬🇧 Khác biệt Mỹ – Anh:

🇺🇸 Tiếng Anh Mỹ
to the hospital
(luôn có "the")
🇬🇧 Tiếng Anh Anh
to hospital
(bệnh nhân nhập viện)
to the hospital
(thăm bệnh nhân)
4.5

Countable vs Uncountable Nouns — Danh Từ Đếm Được & Không Đếm Được

🤔 Mở đầu: Làm sao biết danh từ nào đếm được, nào không?

📊 Bảng so sánh:

Đặc điểm Countable (đếm được) Uncountable (không đếm được)
Mạo từ a the some many the some much
Động từ Số ít hoặc số nhiều Chỉ số ít
Ví dụ a journalist, many articles some news, much information

📝 Danh từ không đếm được thường gặp:

advice coffee furniture glass hair homework information knowledge luggage money news paper work

🔄 Danh từ "hai mặt" — Vừa đếm được vừa không:

Đếm được Không đếm được
a glass (cái ly) glass (chất thủy tinh)
a paper (tờ báo) paper (giấy)
a work (tác phẩm) work (công việc)
⚠️ Danh Từ Số Nhiều Đặc Biệt — Luôn Ở Dạng Số Nhiều

clothes scissors jeans spectacles trousers groceries

My new clothes are dirty!

4.6

Quantifiers — Từ Chỉ Số Lượng

🤔 Mở đầu: "Much" hay "Many"? "Few" hay "Little"?

📊 Bảng tổng hợp:

Quantifier Dùng với Câu Ví dụ
many Countable Phủ định / Nghi vấn There aren't many programmes I like.
much Uncountable Phủ định / Nghi vấn He doesn't show much interest.
a lot of / lots of Cả hai Khẳng định The film won a lot of awards.
a few Countable "Một vài" (tích cực) There have been a few scandals.
few Countable "Ít, không đủ" (tiêu cực) He has few interests.
a little Uncountable "Một chút" (tích cực) They say a little knowledge is dangerous.
little Uncountable "Ít, không đủ" (tiêu cực) The police have little information.

🎯 Phân biệt tinh tế:

A FEW vs FEW:

✅ A Few — Tích cực
I have a few friends.
Tôi có một vài người bạn — đủ, tốt!
⚠️ Few — Tiêu cực
I have few friends.
Tôi có ít bạn — cô đơn, không đủ!

A LITTLE vs LITTLE:

✅ A Little — Tích cực
There's a little sugar left.
Còn một chút đường — đủ dùng!
⚠️ Little — Tiêu cực
There's little sugar left.
Còn rất ít đường — không đủ!
🔥 Cụm Từ Đặc Biệt

There are only a few programmes I like. = not many

There's only a little sugar left. = not much

⚠️ LỖI SAI THƯỜNG GẶP
Lỗi 1 — Nhầm lẫn much / many:
❌How much books do you have?
✅How many books do you have?
Lỗi 2 — Dùng much / many trong câu khẳng định (thân mật):
❌I have many problems.
✅I have a lot of problems.
I have many problems. (formal — văn viết trang trọng)
Lỗi 3 — Nhầm lẫn few / a few:
❌I have few good friends.
✅I have a few good friends.
(khi muốn nói tích cực về số lượng)
💡 Mẹo Ghi Nhớ Siêu Nhanh
  • Much / Many: Much — "u" giống "Uncountable" · Many — "a" giống "mAny objects"
  • A few / A little: Có chữ A = tích cực (có một ít — đủ dùng)
  • Few / Little: Không có A = tiêu cực (quá ít — không đủ)

📖 Exploring My City

Living in a vibrant metropolitan area has taught me that cities are more than just concrete and glass – they're living ecosystems where the past meets the future. My city, nestled between the Rocky Mountains and the Pacific coast, offers a lot of experiences that shape who we are as citizens. There's a unique energy here that you won't find in many other places. The diversity is remarkable, with people from all walks of life contributing to the cultural tapestry. I've discovered that few cities can match ours when it comes to balancing urban development with natural beauty.

The downtown district tells a fascinating story of transformation. Twenty years ago, there were few reasons to visit this area – it had little appeal beyond the basic business functions. Now, journalists write about our urban renewal as a model for other cities. Information about our success travels fast through the media. We have a mayor who understands that progress requires vision, not just money. She's been president of the Urban Development Council for five years, and under her leadership, we've seen much improvement. The French architect who designed our new cultural center said he'd never seen such enthusiasm from a community before.

In the morning, I often walk through Central Park – not the famous one in New York, but our own green oasis. Coffee shops line the streets around it, and there's always a pleasant aroma in the air. I don't need much persuasion to stop for a cup at my favorite café. The owner, an honest and hardworking immigrant, has created a gathering place where locals share news and advice. He doesn't have many tables, just a few cozy corners where regulars sit with their laptops. There's little pretension here – just good coffee and genuine conversation. The atmosphere reminds me that happiness doesn't require wealth.

Our city has several distinct neighborhoods, each with its own character. The arts district attracts creative people who work as photographers, musicians, and writers. They don't earn much money, but they have a little studio space and a lot of passion. Few outsiders understand the dedication these artists show. In the 1990s, this area had few galleries and little cultural activity. Now, on Friday evenings, the streets come alive with music and art. You can play the guitar on street corners or listen to jazz in the clubs. We go to the cinema here too – it's an old theater that shows independent films.

The university district brings a different energy entirely. Students from around the world come here seeking knowledge and opportunities. The library, a masterpiece of modern architecture, contains millions of books and provides information on every subject imaginable. There aren't many places where you can find such resources. A few professors have won Nobel Prizes, bringing prestige to our city. They give advice to young researchers who have little experience but much ambition. The campus spreads across several blocks, and in June, during graduation, families fill the streets with celebration.

Transportation in our city has improved significantly. We have a subway system that, while not perfect, provides reliable service. The buses run frequently, though there's still room for improvement. Many residents prefer cycling – we have miles of bike lanes and a few bike-sharing programs. However, traffic remains a challenge during rush hours. There's little parking downtown, which encourages people to use public transport. The city council has plans to expand the light rail system, but they need more funding. Only a few neighborhoods currently have access to the rail lines.

The culinary scene deserves special mention. We have restaurants representing cuisines from around the globe. The River District offers fine dining where a meal can cost a fortune, but there's also street food that provides delicious options for those with less money. The farmers' market on Saturday mornings brings fresh produce from local farms. Vegetables, fruit, and bread – everything has a quality you won't find in supermarkets. The vendors have a little space each, but they make the most of it. There's an elderly woman who sells honey from her own bees – she has few jars left by noon because the demand is so high.

Shopping here offers variety for every budget. The main shopping street has expensive boutiques where clothes cost more than many people earn in a month. However, there are also thrift stores where you can find bargains. I bought a pair of jeans last week for a fraction of their original price. The vintage shops have furniture and curiosities from past decades. Groceries are available everywhere, from small corner stores to large supermarkets. We have a little of everything, though prices have risen recently. There's much concern about inflation affecting families with limited income.

The cultural offerings make our city special. The museum of natural history has an impressive collection, including fossils from the region. The art gallery showcases both local and international artists. We have a symphony orchestra that performs in the winter, and outdoor concerts in the summer. On television, our local stations provide news and entertainment, though many people now prefer streaming services. The radio still has loyal listeners, especially for morning shows. Sports are huge here – we play tennis in the public courts and football in the parks.

Environmental consciousness shapes our urban planning. The city has regulations about recycling and waste management. We don't have much pollution compared to other cities our size, but there's still work to do. A few neighborhoods have started community gardens where residents grow vegetables without pesticides. The river that runs through downtown was once polluted, but after years of cleanup, it now has fish again. Trees line many streets, providing shade and improving air quality. We have little heavy industry within city limits, which helps maintain the environment.

At night, the city transforms. The lights create a magical atmosphere, especially in December when decorations appear everywhere. The nightlife suits different tastes – from quiet wine bars to energetic dance clubs. However, safety remains a concern in certain areas. The police have little presence in some neighborhoods, leading to problems. Still, most areas are safe, and people walk freely after dark. The sense of community is strong – neighbors look out for each other.

In the end, what makes our city special isn't just the buildings or the infrastructure – it's the people. We have citizens from every background imaginable. Only a few cities can claim such diversity. Yes, we have problems – there's homelessness, inequality, and many challenges ahead. But we also have hope, determination, and a shared vision for the future. A little effort from each person creates much positive change. That's the lesson I've learned from exploring my city: progress happens when people work together, regardless of their differences.

Grammar Features Highlighted

Indefinite Articles (a/an): Used correctly with singular countable nouns (a vibrant metropolitan area, an honest immigrant, a unique energy), paying attention to pronunciation (a university — /ju:/ sound).

Definite Article (the): Used for specific references (the past, the Rocky Mountains, the media), unique things (the mayor), and in fixed expressions (the most).

Zero Article: Correctly used with plural nouns in general (journalists write, students come), uncountable nouns in general (information travels, money doesn't buy happiness), and in fixed expressions (at night, on Friday, in June).

Countable vs Uncountable: Proper distinction maintained (many people vs much money, a few tables vs a little space, clothes are not clothes is).

Quantifiers — all forms used appropriately: many/much in negative/questions (not many places, much improvement); a lot of/lots of in affirmatives (a lot of experiences); few/a few showing negative/positive connotations (few cities = not many, a few professors = some); little/a little showing insufficient/sufficient (little appeal = not enough, a little space = some).

Fixed Expressions: Correct usage of articles in set phrases (in the morning, at night, play the guitar, play tennis, go to the cinema, on television).

Special Cases: Proper nouns correctly handled (the Rocky Mountains but Central Park), positions (work as photographers, been president), and American English preferences (to the hospital).
