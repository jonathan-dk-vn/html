Unit 12 · Destination B2

RELATIVE CLAUSES
Nghệ Thuật Nối Câu

So sánh: "I met a man. The man was very tall." vs "I met a man who was very tall." — Relative Clauses giúp câu nói trở nên mượt mà, tiết kiệm và chuyên nghiệp hơn!

12.1

Defining Relative Clauses — Mệnh Đề Quan Hệ Xác Định

🤔 Mở đầu: Làm sao phân biệt "cái nào" trong đám đông?
Bản chất: Defining relative clauses giúp xác định cụ thể người/vật nào chúng ta đang nói đến. Không có chúng, câu sẽ không rõ nghĩa!

📝 Các đại từ quan hệ:

Đại từ Dùng cho Chức năng Ví dụ
which Vật, động vật Chủ ngữ / Tân ngữ The film which was on TV
who Người Chủ ngữ The man who is tall
whom Người Tân ngữ (trang trọng) The man whom we saw
whose Người / Vật Sở hữu The girl whose bag is red
when Thời gian Trạng từ The day when we met
where Nơi chốn Trạng từ The place where I was born
why Lý do Trạng từ The reason why he left

🎯 Ví dụ chi tiết:

  • WHICH (cho vật): Did you see the film which was on TV last night? Phân tích: Bộ phim NÀO? → Bộ phim đã chiếu trên TV!
  • WHO (cho người): Tom Davies, who is appearing in concert, is with me. Phân tích: Tom Davies NÀO? → Tom Davies đang biểu diễn!
  • WHOSE (sở hữu): My next guest is John Travolta, whose career goes back to the 70s. Phân tích: Sự nghiệp của John Travolta!
  • WHERE (thay thế "in which"): The theatre where I first acted… = The theatre which I first acted in… (giới từ cuối câu)

🔥 Đặc điểm quan trọng:

  • 1. Không có dấu phẩy: This is the DVD which I told you about. ✅ Đúng — Không có dấu phẩy!
  • 2. Có thể bỏ đại từ quan hệ khi làm tân ngữ: This is the DVD (which) I told you about. Khi đại từ là tân ngữ → có thể bỏ!
  • 3. Không dùng THAT trong non-defining: The book which is on the table… ✅ Defining — OK. Nhưng "My book, that is red…" ❌ Non-defining không dùng THAT!
⚠️ LỖI SAI THƯỜNG GẶP
Lỗi 1 — Thêm chủ ngữ thừa:
❌The man who he is tall…
✅The man who is tall…
Lỗi 2 — Nhầm lẫn WHO vs WHOM:
❌The man whom helped me…
✅The man who helped me.
(WHO = chủ ngữ — là người giúp)
✅The man whom I helped.
(WHOM = tân ngữ — là người được giúp)
12.2

Non-Defining Relative Clauses — Mệnh Đề Quan Hệ Không Xác Định

🤔 Mở đầu: Khi nào thông tin chỉ "bổ sung thêm"?
Bản chất: Non-defining relative clauses chỉ bổ sung thông tin — bỏ đi vẫn hiểu câu!

🎯 Đặc điểm:

  • 1. Có dấu phẩy: Ray Watson, who starred in Bandits, is considering a new film.
  • 2. Không thể bỏ đại từ quan hệ: Megamonsters, which was filmed in New York, is disappointing. ❌ Không được bỏ "which"!
  • 3. Không dùng THAT: My car, which is red, is expensive. ❌ "My car, that is red…" — THAT không dùng trong non-defining!
🔥 "WHICH" Đặc Biệt — Thay Thế Cả Câu

We got tickets for the concert, which was very lucky.

Phân tích: "which" = việc chúng ta có được vé (không phải "concert")

🔄 So sánh Defining vs Non-Defining:

Defining — Xác Định
Không dấu phẩy · Có thể bỏ ĐTV · Có thể dùng THAT
Students who work hard succeed.
Sinh viên NÀO? → Sinh viên chăm chỉ
Non-Defining — Bổ Sung
Có dấu phẩy · Không bỏ ĐTV · Không dùng THAT
My brother, who lives in London, is a doctor.
Thông tin thêm về anh trai
Đặc điểm Defining Non-Defining
Xác định / Bổ sung Xác định người/vật cụ thể Bổ sung thông tin
Dấu phẩy ❌ Không có ✅ Có
Bỏ đại từ ✅ Có thể bỏ (nếu là tân ngữ) ❌ Không thể bỏ
Dùng THAT ✅ Có thể ❌ Không
12.3

Participles — Phân Từ (Rút Gọn Mệnh Đề Quan Hệ)

🤔 Mở đầu: Cách "rút gọn" relative clauses?
Bản chất: Participles giúp rút gọn câu, tránh lặp lại và tạo sự mượt mà.

📝 Ba loại Participles:

Present · V-ing WHO/WHICH + be + V-ing The person directing the film = The person who is directing the film
Past · V3 WHO/WHICH + be + V3 The person chosen for the part = The person who is chosen…
Perfect · having+V3 WHO/WHICH + have + V3 After having finished homework = After I had finished…

🎯 Các cách dùng:

  • 1. Sau giới từ và liên từ: By appearing in that advert, he damaged his career. After playing video games, I was tired.
  • 2. Giải thích lý do: Being quite good looking, Ralph decided to model. Having seen the film before, I knew the ending.
  • 3. Hành động đồng thời: Waiting for the show, I felt nervous.
  • 4. Thay thế relative clauses: Imagine being the person directing a film! The person chosen will be contacted.
  • 5. Hành động liên tiếp: Having finished homework, I went to cinema.
  • 6. Thể bị động khác: Made to wait, the actor got annoyed.
⚠️ LỖI SAI THƯỜNG GẶP
Lỗi 1 — Sai chủ ngữ (Dangling Participle):
❌Watching TV, a news report came on.
✅Watching TV, I saw a news report.
Chủ ngữ của participle và mệnh đề chính PHẢI giống nhau!
Lỗi 2 — Nhầm lẫn thời gian:
❌Having finished homework, I watch TV.
✅Having finished homework, I watched TV.
Cả hai hành động trong quá khứ → phải dùng thì quá khứ
Lỗi 3 — Sai chủ ngữ (lỗi phổ biến):
❌Having moved to a new town, my confidence was low.
✅Having moved to a new town, I felt my confidence was low.
Participle "having moved" phải có cùng chủ ngữ với mệnh đề chính
💡 Mẹo Ghi Nhớ
  • Present Participle (V-ing): Hành động chủ động, đồng thời
  • Past Participle (V3): Hành động bị động
  • Perfect Participle (Having V3): Hành động trước một hành động khác
  • Chủ ngữ phải giống nhau! (quy tắc quan trọng nhất)

📖 The People and Places That Define Me

Our identities are not formed in a vacuum; they are mosaics, pieced together from the people who love us and the places where we feel at home. When I reflect on my life, I see a collection of faces and landscapes that have shaped the person I am today. These are not just abstract memories; they are the defining elements, the anchors in my story.

My hometown is a place that will always be a part of me. I grew up in a small house which my parents bought just after they got married. I can still recall the exact day when we moved in. The park where I played as a child, however, holds the most significant memories. This park, which is now a designated historic landmark, was my sanctuary. It was the place for adventures with friends, the people with whom I shared my childhood secrets. The specific oak tree under which we used to sit still stands there today. We eventually left that town, which was a difficult transition for a teenager. That move was a moment that taught me about change and resilience.

Beyond places, certain individuals have left an indelible mark on my life. There was my high school English teacher, Mr. Harrison, who was the first person to encourage my writing. The feedback (that) he gave me was invaluable. Then there is my best friend, Anna, whose unwavering support has guided me through my toughest times. Anna, who is now a successful lawyer in Chicago, remains the person I can call at any hour. It is these people, the ones who see the best in us, that truly define our journey. I've learned that a true friend is someone who believes in you even when you've stopped believing in yourself.

As I've grown older, I've learned to describe these memories in a more fluid style using participles. Instead of saying "the teacher who inspired me," I might think of him as "the teacher inspiring me to write." I can picture Anna, laughing loudly at a story I'm telling, her joy being infectious. The advice given by Mr. Harrison still echoes in my mind. Being a naturally shy person, I found it hard to make friends after we moved. However, encouraged by my parents, I joined the school's drama club. Having decided to take that risk, I discovered a passion for acting and met a whole new group of creative people. It's crucial to remember that the subject of the participle must match the main clause. It would be incorrect to say, "Having moved to a new town, my confidence was low." The correct structure is, "Having moved to a new town, I felt my confidence was low."

In conclusion, the people and places of our past are more than just stories. The town where I grew up and the teacher who mentored me are integral threads in the fabric of my being. My best friend Anna, whose loyalty is a gift, continues to be a guiding light. Looking back, I realize how these specific, defining connections have built my world. Surrounded by such positive influences, I have been incredibly fortunate. These are the people and places that make me who I am.

Grammar Features Highlighted

Defining Relative Clauses: Used to provide essential information with who, which, that, where, when, and whom. Examples: the people who love us; the place where we feel at home; a small house which my parents bought; the day when we moved in; the person I am today.

Pronoun Omission: Demonstrates omitting the relative pronoun when it is the object — The feedback (that) he gave me…

Whom: Correctly used as the object of a preposition — the people with whom I shared…; the oak tree under which we used to sit…

Non-Defining Relative Clauses: Used to provide extra information, correctly set off by commas — This park, which is now a designated historic landmark, was my sanctuary; Anna, who is now a successful lawyer in Chicago, remains my best friend.

'Which' referring to a whole clause: We eventually left that town, which was a difficult transition… — "which" refers to the entire act of leaving.

'Whose' in non-defining context: Anna, whose unwavering support has guided me…

Present Participle (-ing): Used for active actions and providing reasons — the teacher inspiring me to write; Anna, laughing loudly…; Being a naturally shy person…

Past Participle (V3): Used for passive actions — The advice given by Mr. Harrison; Encouraged by my parents…; Surrounded by such positive influences…

Perfect Participle (Having + V3): Used for an action that happened before the main action — Having decided to take that risk, I discovered…

Common Errors Avoided: The essay explicitly corrects a dangling participle error, ensuring the subject of the participle and the main clause match; no double subjects in relative clauses; correct use of who (subject) vs whom (object); THAT not used in non-defining clauses.
