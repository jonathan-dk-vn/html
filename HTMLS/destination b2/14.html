14.1

Possessive 'S and S' — Sở Hữu Cách

🤔 Mở đầu: "John's car" hay "the car of John"?
Bản chất: Sở hữu cách thể hiện mối quan hệ sở hữu một cách ngắn gọn và tự nhiên.

📝 Quy tắc cơ bản:

Loại danh từ Cách viết Ví dụ
Số ít 's the manager's car · my sister's book
Số nhiều kết thúc -s ' (chỉ dấu phẩy) the workers' pay · my parents' house
Số nhiều không kết thúc -s 's the children's toys · women's rights

🕐 Biểu hiện thời gian:

  • Thời gian + 's: After an hour's wait, I saw the manager.
  • Tuần, ngày: I need a week's holiday. · It's a ten minutes' walk.

🎯 Khi nào dùng 's / Khi nào dùng OF:

Dùng 'S / S' Dùng OF
✅ Người và động vật
the company's finances
the cat's paw		 ✅ Đồ vật
the back of the computer
the top of the bottle

🔥 Trường hợp đặc biệt:

  • Tên riêng kết thúc bằng -s: Charles' computer hoặc Charles's computer Cả hai đều đúng!
  • Tên gia đình số nhiều: The Smiths' business Gia đình Smith
  • Cụm từ dài — 's ở cuối cùng: Tom, Dick and Harry's office 's chỉ ở người cuối cùng trong chuỗi!
  • Cụm từ chức vụ — 's ở cuối cụm: The President of France's visit
⚠️ LỖI SAI THƯỜNG GẶP
Lỗi 1 — Đặt sai vị trí 's:
❌Tom's and Dick's and Harry's office
✅Tom, Dick and Harry's office
Lỗi 2 — Dùng 's với đồ vật:
❌The car's engine
✅The engine of the car
Lỗi 3 — Nhầm lẫn sở hữu với số nhiều:
❌The boy's are playing.
✅The boys are playing.
✅The boy's toys are here.
(đồ chơi của cậu bé)
14.2

Possessive Determiners & Pronouns — My vs Mine

🤔 Mở đầu: "My book" vs "Mine" — Khi nào dùng cái nào?

📊 Bảng so sánh:

Determiner
my
mine
Determiner
your
yours
Determiner
his
his
Determiner
her
hers
Determiner
its
— (không có)
Determiner
our
ours
Determiner
their
theirs

🎯 Cách dùng:

  • Possessive Determiners (+ noun): I'm excited about my new job.
  • Possessive Pronouns (thay thế noun): My job is great. How about yours? · This book is mine.
  • Sau "of": She's a colleague of mine. · That friend of yours is funny.
🔥 ITS vs IT'S — Hai thứ hoàn toàn khác nhau!
  • The company increased its profits. (sở hữu — its = of it)
  • It's a great company. (it is — viết tắt)
⚠️ LỖI SAI THƯỜNG GẶP
Lỗi 1 — Nhầm determiner vs pronoun:
❌Mine book is here.
✅My book is here.
❌How about your?
✅How about yours?
Lỗi 2 — Thêm apostrophe vào pronoun:
❌The book is their's.
✅The book is theirs.
14.3

Inversions with Negative Adverbials — Đảo Ngữ Nhấn Mạnh

🤔 Mở đầu: Tại sao đảo ngữ làm câu nói "sang chảnh"?
Bản chất: Inversions tạo hiệu ứng nhấn mạnh và thể hiện trình độ cao — như "đũa phép" của tiếng Anh!

📝 Quy tắc chung:

Negative adverbial + auxiliary + subject + main verb

📊 Các từ/cụm từ thường dùng:

Never Never have I worked so hard.
Rarely / Seldom Rarely have I seen such talent.
No sooner…than No sooner had he arrived than it started raining.
Hardly…when Hardly had I sat down when the phone rang.
Not only…but also Not only is he smart, but he's also funny.
Under no circumstances Under no circumstances should you give up.
At no time/point At no time was I told about this.
Little Little did I know how hard it would be. = I didn't know at all — dùng với know, realise, suspect, understand
Not until Not until midnight did he return home.
Only when Only when I met her did I understand love.

🔥 Phân tích chi tiết — NOT UNTIL / ONLY:

⚡ Chú ý — Đảo ngữ ở mệnh đề nào?

Đảo ngữ MỆNH ĐỀ CHÍNH (sau "Not until…"):

Not until I finished homework was I allowed to go out.

= Trật tự bình thường trong mệnh đề thời gian, đảo ngữ trong mệnh đề kết quả!

🎯 Ví dụ so sánh bình thường vs đảo ngữ:

Câu bình thường Câu đảo ngữ (trang trọng hơn)
I have never worked so hard in my life. Never have I worked so hard in my life.
He had no sooner arrived than it rained. No sooner had he arrived than it rained.
⚠️ LỖI SAI THƯỜNG GẶP
Lỗi 1 — Quên đảo ngữ:
❌Never I have seen such beauty.
✅Never have I seen such beauty.
Lỗi 2 — Sai trật tự với NOT UNTIL:
❌Not until he had finished was allowed to leave.
✅Not until he had finished was he allowed to leave.
14.4

Other Inversions — Đảo Ngữ Khác

1. Short answers với SO / NEITHER / NOR:

Câu khẳng định → SO
"I'm a plumber!"
"Really? So am I!"
"I like coffee."
"So do I."
Câu phủ định → NEITHER/NOR
"I don't like coffee."
"Neither do I." / "Nor do I."
"I can't swim."
"Neither can I."

2. Sau AS / SO / SUCH:

  • Sau AS: The manager was nervous, as were the staff.
  • Sau SUCH (nhấn mạnh): Such a hot day was it that no one worked.

3. Conditional inversions (bỏ "if"):

Có "if" (thường) Không có "if" (trang trọng)
If I were you, I would be careful. Were I you, I would be careful.
If I had known, I would have come. Had I known, I would have come.
⚠️ LỖI SAI THƯỜNG GẶP
Lỗi — Nhầm lẫn so và neither:
❌"I don't like it." "So do I."
✅"I don't like it." "Neither do I."
Câu phủ định → dùng NEITHER/NOR (không dùng SO)
💡 Mẹo Ghi Nhớ
  • SO: Đồng ý với câu khẳng định
  • NEITHER / NOR: Đồng ý với câu phủ định
  • Conditional inversions: Were I / Had I / Should I — bỏ "if", đảo Were/Had/Should lên đầu

📖 The Unwritten Rules: My Family's Traditions

Every family has its own unique culture, a set of traditions that are rarely written down but are understood by all. In my family, these traditions are the anchors of our year, shaping our gatherings and strengthening our bonds. Never have I seen these customs broken; they are the unwritten rules that define us. From the specific roles each person plays to the timing of every event, these moments are not just habits but cherished ceremonies.

The ownership of tasks and memories is clearly, if informally, defined. My grandmother's kitchen is the heart of all our celebrations. Her recipes are a closely guarded secret, and the results are a testament to a lifetime's practice. During the holidays, my parents' main job is decoration. The festive atmosphere they create is magical for the children's enjoyment. The most significant event is my Aunt Jane and Uncle Tom's anniversary party. The responsibility for the music is always my brother's, while the task of organizing the guest list is mine. The house itself belongs to them, but on that day, the joy is ours to share. A friend of yours might find our structured approach amusing, but for us, it's a beautifully coordinated dance.

Our traditions are governed by rules that are communicated with emphatic gravity. Under no circumstances is anyone late for the annual family photo. Not only is this picture a historical record, but it is also a source of great pride for my grandfather. As children, little did we understand the importance of these moments. We just knew the rules. No sooner had the photo been taken than we were allowed to run off and play. I particularly remember the rule about dessert: not until everyone had finished the main course was the cake allowed to be cut. This structure, inverting the main clause, perfectly captures the dramatic weight of the moment. Rarely have I tasted anything as delicious as my grandmother's chocolate cake after a long, patient wait.

These traditions create a powerful sense of unity and shared feeling. My father loves telling old family stories. "So does my uncle." My younger cousins, however, don't enjoy sitting still for so long. "Neither do I, if I'm being honest," my sister once whispered to me. The bond is palpable. Such was the feeling of togetherness that even newcomers felt instantly welcome. Reflecting on it now, I see the past with new eyes. Were I to have my own family one day, I would continue these traditions. Had I known then how fast time would pass, I would have cherished each moment even more. These aren't just my family's traditions; they are a legacy.

In the end, these rituals are more than just repeated actions. They are the language of our family's love. They are the back of the photo album, not the photo album's back. They belong to the Jameses' history, a story passed down through generations. The traditions are not just his or hers; they are theirs, ours, and a precious inheritance for the future. Seldom does a family create something so strong out of such simple, repeated acts of love and gathering.

Grammar Features Highlighted

Possessive 's and s': Correctly used with singular (grandmother's), plural (parents'), and irregular plural (children's) nouns; time expression (a lifetime's practice); long phrase (Aunt Jane and Uncle Tom's); family name (the Jameses' history).

Of vs 's: The essay correctly uses the back of the photo album (not "the photo album's back") — demonstrating the rule that inanimate objects take "of" not "'s."

Possessive Determiners vs Pronouns: my brother's (role + noun), mine (pronoun replacing noun), ours (pronoun), yours (after "of"), his, hers, theirs — all correctly used.

Its vs It's: Correctly uses its for possession (Every family has its own unique culture) and it's for "it is" (it's a beautifully coordinated dance).

Negative Adverbial Inversions: Never have I seen…; Under no circumstances is anyone late; Not only is this picture…, but it is also…; Little did we understand…; No sooner had the photo been taken than…; Not until everyone had finished…was the cake allowed… (correctly inverting the main clause); Rarely have I tasted…; Seldom does a family create…

Other Inversions: So/Neither (So does my uncle; Neither do I); Conditional inversions (Were I to have…; Had I known…); Such…that (Such was the feeling of togetherness that…).

Common Errors Avoided: No double apostrophe in possessive pronouns (theirs, ours, not their's/our's); correct word order after negative adverbials; correct "Neither" (not "So") for negative responses; proper inversion in main clause for "Not until"; no use of 's with inanimate compound objects.
